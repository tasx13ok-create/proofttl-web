# ProofTTL Fact Audit — Complyze

Audit date: 2026-08-30
Target surface: Complyze public Live Regulatory Feed
Target page: https://www.complyze.dev/
Status: OUTREACH-READY EVIDENCE PACKET

## Executive read

Complyze markets its regulatory intelligence as grounded in real, timestamped data and its AI compliance assistant as giving cited answers grounded in live regulatory data with "zero hallucinations." Its public feed currently contains a high-confidence timing error on the EU AI Act that is especially material because the governing timetable changed in July 2026.

The strongest outbound finding is that Complyze says high-risk AI systems "must now comply with conformity assessment requirements" as of August 2, 2026. The current consolidated EU AI Act and European Commission guidance say the relevant high-risk obligations apply later: December 2, 2027 for Annex III systems and August 2, 2028 for Annex I product systems.

## Finding 1 — High-risk EU AI Act obligations shown as already applicable

Severity: HIGH
Verdict: CONTRADICTED / STALE
Confidence: HIGH

### Published claim

Complyze's public Live Regulatory Feed says:

> "EU AI Act Enters Full Application Phase"

and states that on August 2, 2026, "High-risk AI systems must now comply with conformity assessment requirements."

The page says its regulatory data was verified April 4, 2026.

### Primary / authoritative evidence

Consolidated Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Article 113, version incorporating the July 27, 2026 amendment:
https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/2026-07-27/eng

Article 113 now provides that Chapter III Sections 1–3 (the high-risk system framework, subject to the listed exception) apply:
- December 2, 2027 for Article 6(2) / Annex III high-risk systems; and
- August 2, 2028 for Article 6(1) / Annex I product high-risk systems.

European Commission AI Act guidance likewise states that high-risk rules begin December 2, 2027, while high-risk AI embedded in regulated physical products begins August 2, 2028:
https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/navigating-ai-act

The Commission's current AI Act overview confirms the same timetable:
https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai

### Why the claim fails

August 2, 2026 is an important AI Act application/enforcement milestone, including Article 50 transparency rules and Commission enforcement powers. It is not, however, the date on which the main high-risk conformity-assessment regime became generally applicable under the current law.

The Complyze sentence therefore tells compliance users that a major obligation is already live when the legally operative dates are more than a year later for Annex III systems and nearly two years later for Annex I systems.

### User consequence

A compliance team relying on the feed could misstate its present legal obligations, accelerate or prioritize the wrong compliance work, communicate an incorrect regulatory deadline internally or to customers, or incorrectly classify a system as presently subject to conformity-assessment requirements.

### Repair

Replace the high-risk sentence with a phased timeline that distinguishes:
- August 2, 2026: most remaining AI Act provisions, Article 50 transparency obligations, and enforcement milestones;
- December 2, 2027: Annex III high-risk requirements; and
- August 2, 2028: Annex I/product high-risk requirements.

Also re-verify any cached regulatory records after material amendments instead of relying on an April 2026 verification timestamp.

## Finding 2 — "Full application" framing obscures material delayed provisions

Severity: MEDIUM
Verdict: OVERSTATED / TIMELINE SCOPE ERROR
Confidence: HIGH

### Published claim

The feed headline says "EU AI Act Enters Full Application Phase" on August 2, 2026.

### Evidence

The current consolidated Article 113 expressly carries later application dates for core high-risk provisions. The European Commission also describes August 2, 2026 as the date the Act became applicable "with some exceptions," then lists high-risk rules beginning in 2027 and 2028.

### Why the claim needs qualification

"Full application" is materially misleading in a compliance product when entire high-risk regulatory sections remain subject to later application dates. A general news headline might tolerate shorthand; a product sold as regulatory intelligence should expose the exceptions that drive compliance decisions.

### Repair

Use "major application and enforcement milestone" rather than "full application," and surface the delayed high-risk dates directly in the feed card.

## Finding 3 — DORA status label uses the wrong legal milestone

Severity: LOW / MEDIUM
Verdict: STATUS TERMINOLOGY ERROR
Confidence: HIGH

### Published claim

Complyze labels a feed item "EU DORA Enters Into Force" and ties it to January 17, 2025.

### Authoritative evidence

Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 (DORA) entered into force on January 16, 2023 and applied from January 17, 2025. The January 2025 date is the application date, not the entry-into-force date.

Primary regulation:
https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2022/2554/oj

### Why it matters

For regulatory intelligence, "entry into force" and "date of application" are distinct legal states. The body text correctly describes January 17, 2025 as the date DORA became applicable, but the status headline mislabels the event.

### Repair

Rename the event to "EU DORA Becomes Applicable" and reserve "entry into force" for January 16, 2023.
